Abstract 
In this course, we will take a detailed look at diﬀerent topics in the ﬁeld of 3D user interfaces
 
 
 
 
 
   
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
(3DUIs) for Virtual Reality and Gaming. With the advent of Augmented and Virtual Reality in
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
numerous applicaƟon areas, the need and interest in more eﬀecƟve interfaces becomes prevalent,
 
 
 
 
 
 
   
 
 
 
 
 
among others driven forward by improved technologies, increasing applicaƟon complexity and
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
user experience requirements. Within this course, we highlight key issues in the design of diverse
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
3DUIs by looking closely into both simple and advanced 3D selecƟon/manipulaƟon and spaƟal
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
navigaƟon interface design topics. These topics are highly relevant, as they form the basis for most
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
3DUI‐driven applicaƟon, yet also can cause major issues (performance, usability, experience.
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
moƟon sickness) when not designed properly as they can be diﬃcult to handle. Within this course,
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
we build on top of a general understanding of 3DUIs to discuss typical piƞalls by looking closely at
 
 
 
 
   
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
theoreƟcal and pracƟcal aspects of selecƟon, manipulaƟon, and navigaƟon and highlight
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
guidelines for their use.

Course structure 
Both the selecƟon and manipulaƟon and the spaƟal navigaƟon blocks deal with foundaƟons and
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
pracƟcal aspects to create a solid foundaƟon for creaƟng 3D user interfaces. While other 3D user

Both the 3D SelecƟon and ManipulaƟon and the spaƟal navigaƟon blocks will start by covering the

moƟon sickness and usability issues will be discussed, along with a set of guidelines to alleviate

In the subsequent more applied  pracƟce secƟon of each block, we will provide an overview of the

and validaƟon methodologies.

Color blindness
• Depending on country 7-10% of men and 
0.4% of women experience inherited color 
deficiency.
• Color blindness is sex-linked: genes that 
produce photopigments are carried on the 
X-chromosome. 
Males have only one X-chromosome 
(females have two) and are more prone to 
defects (for females the faulty gene must 
be present on both X-chromosomes).
• Three types of color blindness: 
• Monochromacy (0.0001% males, 
all cones disfunction). 
• Dichromacy (2.4% males, one cone 
disfunctions, mostly red or green).
