18
Type A: 
A sophisticated implementation of something that solves a mobile 
interaction problem in a unique or good/better way plus a small user 
study (e.g., usability) or other verification (e.g., proof of concept)
Type B: 
A small implementation of a mobile interaction approach or technique 
plus a detailed evaluation
Example from previous years: environment-dependent notification system 
(phone senses user status & environment & adapts notifications accordingly)
Example from previous years: a small game where you steer the character 
either by tilting the device or via on-screen joystick (plus a comparative user 
study evaluating performance and game play experience)
Note: this can be very simple (if you 
want to learn mobile programming) 
or very advanced (if you have experience 
and want to improve your skills)
Note that you could also test small screen design aspects on a PC if you don’t want to program mobile.
Type C: 
A detailed evaluation of different mobile interaction approaches 
with existing apps
E.g., tilting vs. joystick 
(see example for topic B)

How Apple defines the “Retina Display”:
The term retina is justified based on the assertion that a display of 
approximately 300 ppi at a distance of 12 inches (305 mm) from one’s 
eye is the maximum amount of detail that the human retina can 
perceive. With the iPhone expected to be used at a distance of about 
12 inches from the eyes, a higher resolution would allegedly have no 
effect on the image's apparent quality as the maximum potential of the 
human eye has already been met.

Experimental research
16
Confounding / extraneous variables = variables other than the independent 
variables that might affect the dependent variable
• They always exist; influence on results needs to be eliminated / minimized.
Confounding variables have an impact on validity:
• When controlled: higher confidence on causality → high internal validity
• When left as random: reflect variation in natural use → high external validity
Validity is a term describing the relevance and reliability of a result:
• Internal validity: to what degree can we assume that 
the effect does indeed result from the change of the independent variables?
• External validity: to what degree can we generalize our results 
to general conditions other than the ones under which we tested?

AR definition by Azuma
REALITY
AR
VR
“To avoid limiting AR to specific technologies, 
this survey defines AR as systems that have 
the following three characteristics:
1.
Combines real and virtual
2.
Registered in 3-D (*)
3.
Interactive in real time
This definition allows other technologies 
besides HMDs while retaining the essential 
components of AR.”
R. Azuma, A Survey of Augmented Reality. 
Presence: Teleoperators and Virtual Environments, 
pp. 355–385, Aug 1997.
(*) The process of correctly aligning the virtual world with the 
virtual one is called 3d registration. To perfectly merge the 
virtual objects generated by computer with the real world image 
from the camera, 3d information about the enviroment as well as 
accurate localization of the camera in a world coordinate frame.
